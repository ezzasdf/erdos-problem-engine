## Scope of a commercial license

A license is granted per organization, in writing, and nothing is licensed
until both parties sign. Each agreement states, in its own terms:

- **Permitted uses.** Which products, services, or internal systems may use the
  code, and whether that use is production, evaluation, or research only.
- **Modification rights.** Whether you may change the code, and whether
  modifications may be kept private or must be disclosed.
- **Redistribution rights.** Whether binaries or modified sources may be shipped
  to end users, embedded in a product, or sublicensed, and on what terms.
- **Fee.** The amount, currency, payment schedule, and whether it is a one-time
  payment, a subscription, or a royalty.
- **Term.** Whether the grant is perpetual or time-limited, and the renewal
  conditions if it is limited.
- **Termination.** What ends the agreement, what happens on breach or
  non-payment, and how long the licensee has to stop using the code afterwards.
- **Warranty and liability.** The code is provided as is; any warranty or
  indemnity is a separate negotiated term, not a default.

It does not transfer copyright, and it does not grant trademark rights.
