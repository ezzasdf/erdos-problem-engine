## The model

Noncommercial use, including use by universities, public research
organizations, and government institutions, is free. PolyForm Strict 1.0.0
implements that through its own noncommercial definition. Private companies
using the code commercially pay. `COMMERCIAL_AGREEMENT_TEMPLATE.md` is the
document that sells that use.

Academics and noncommercial users do not need a license, but PolyForm Strict
does not permit them to modify the code either. If you are one of those users
and want to modify it for your own research, write to the address below;
permission is granted free, in writing, and normally the same day.

If you want to do any of the following, and you are not one of the
organizations listed in the next section, you need a commercial license:

- use the code, in whole or in part, in a commercial product or service;
- deliver the code or work built on it to a paying client;
- modify it, extend it, or build on it;
- redistribute it, with or without changes, inside an organization or to
  third parties;
- use it to train, evaluate, or benchmark a commercial model or system.

## What the public license already permits

PolyForm Strict 1.0.0 defines what counts as a noncommercial purpose, and that
definition includes use by a charitable organization, educational institution,
public research organization, public safety or health organization,
environmental protection organization, or government institution, **regardless
of the source of funding or obligations resulting from the funding**.

A university running industry-funded research, or a public research institute
doing contract work, therefore already falls inside the public permission. This
is a limit on the offer below, not a marketing detail: a license is needed only
for use outside that definition.

## Scope of a commercial license

A license is granted per organization, in writing, and nothing is licensed
until both parties sign. Each agreement states, in its own terms:

- **Permitted uses.** Which products, services, or internal systems may use the
  code, and whether that use is production, evaluation, or research only.
- **Modification rights.** Whether you may change the code, and whether
  modifications may be kept private or must be disclosed.
- **Redistribution rights.** Whether binaries or modified sources may be shipped
  to end users, embedded in a product, or sublicensed, and on what terms.
- **Fee.** The amount, currency, payment schedule, and whether it is a one-time
  payment, a subscription, or a royalty.
- **Term.** Whether the grant is perpetual or time-limited, and the renewal
  conditions if it is limited.
- **Termination.** What ends the agreement, what happens on breach or
  non-payment, and how long the licensee has to stop using the code afterwards.
- **Warranty and liability.** The code is provided as is; any warranty or
  indemnity is a separate negotiated term, not a default.

It does not transfer copyright, and it does not grant trademark rights.

## Have the agreement reviewed

These headings describe what an agreement should cover; they are not a contract
template. Before selling the first license, have the signed form drafted or
reviewed by a lawyer who works with software licensing in your jurisdiction, and
check three things in particular:

- how it interacts with the Apache-2.0 grant that already covers earlier
  versions;
- where the noncommercial boundary actually falls, since PolyForm Strict 1.0.0
  already permits use by educational and public research organizations
  regardless of funding;
- whether the fee, term, and termination clauses survive a change of control or
  a subcontractor.
