### Abstract

For any integer multiplier $M$ generated by a digitwise-safe polynomial, the Hausdorff dimension of the ternary intersection set $C(1, M) = C \cap M^{-1}(C)$ satisfies $\dim_H C(1, M) \le \log_3 \phi \approx 0.438018$, strictly improving Lagarias's universal upper bound $1/2$. We prove that equality $\dim_H C(1, M) = \log_3 \phi$ holds if and only if $M \in \{3^k + 1 : k \ge 1\} \cup \{3^{2k} - 3^k + 1 : k \ge 1\}$, identifying a completely new second infinite family of extremizers whose prefix counts match Fibonacci powers $F_{n+2}^k$. We formalize the core theory in Lean 4: boundary cases $a = 1$ and $b - a = 1$ are kernel-verified (the latter via a new time-reversal duality theorem $P M_{(a, b)} P = M_{(b-a, b)}^T$), while the interior domain $b - a \ge 2$ is established in prose via the Parry measure on an explicit universal $2b$-periodic orbit, with its discrete dynamical foundations verified in Lean 4 modulo the Birkhoff ergodic theorem; the classical Erdős ternary conjecture remains open.

## 0. Notation & Standing Conventions

Let $\Sigma = \{x \in \mathbb{Z}_3 : \text{every ternary digit of } x \text{ is } 0 \text{ or } 1\}$ denote the "digitally sum-free" middle-thirds Cantor set of Lagarias (under the standard embedding $\Sigma \subset [0, 1]$ scaled by $1/2$ to $\{0, 2\}^\mathbb{N}$), and let
$$C(1, M) = \{ x \in \Sigma : M x \in \Sigma \}$$
denote the set of ternary reals in $\Sigma$ whose dilate by $M$ remains entirely within $\Sigma$.

Write $\phi = (1 + \sqrt{5})/2$ for the golden ratio, and $\log_3 x = \log x / \log 3$.
For pure 3-powers $M = 3^m$, $C(1, M) = \Sigma$ trivially with dimension $\log_3 2 \approx 0.6309298$ (Lagarias 2009).
Throughout, "safe" refers to Condition (S) defined below.

## 1. Introduction & Statement of Results

### 1.1 Motivation and Background

In 1978, Paul Erdős famously conjectured that $2^0 = 1, 2^2 = 4, 2^8 = 256$ are the only powers of 2 whose ternary expansion omits the digit 2. In his seminal 2009 work, Jeffrey C. Lagarias introduced the multiplier intersection sets $C(1, M) = C \cap M^{-1}(C)$ as a continuous dynamical lens on this problem. Lagarias proved that whenever $M$ is not a power of 3, the Hausdorff dimension satisfies $\dim_H C(1, M) \le 1/2$. Moreover, for $M = 3^k + 1$ (such as $M = 4, 10, 28$), Lagarias proved that $C(1, M)$ is isomorphic to a golden-mean shift of finite type (SFT) with exact Hausdorff dimension:
$$\dim_H C(1, 3^k + 1) = \log_3 \phi = 0.438017879486\dots < \frac{1}{2}.$$
Lagarias conjectured that $\log_3 \phi$ represents the maximal possible dimension for all non-3-powers, and asked whether any other integer multipliers could attain this bound.

### 1.2 Our Main Contributions

In this paper, we resolve these questions on the entire "digitwise-safe" class of multipliers:

1. **Discovery of the Second Extremal Family (Theorem 1):** We prove that multipliers of the form $M = 3^{2k} - 3^k + 1$ ($M = 7, 73, 703, \dots$) achieve exact Hausdorff dimension $\log_3 \phi$, with prefix counts identically matching Fibonacci powers $F_{n+2}^k$.
2. **Digitwise Factorization & Dimension Bridge (Theorem 2):** We prove that under Condition (S), the multi-digit dynamical system decomposes into $k$ independent 1D shifts of finite type with zero carries, and prove via Frostman's Lemma that $\dim_H C(1, M) = \log_3 \lambda(P)$.
3. **Classification of the Safe Class (Theorem 3):** We prove that polynomials satisfying Condition (S) with $P(3^k) > 0$ consist uniquely of 3-powers, Family 1 ($M = 3^a + 1$), and Family 2 ($M = 3^b - 3^a + 1$ with $b > a \ge 1$).
4. **Sharpness & Extremizer Characterization (Theorem 4):** We prove that $\dim_H C(1, M) \le \log_3 \phi$ universally on the safe class, with equality holding if and only if $M \in \text{Family 1} \cup \text{Family 2 with } b = 2a$. All other safe multipliers satisfy $\dim_H C(1, M) < \log_3 \phi$ strictly.
5. **Machine Verification & Time-Reversal Duality (Theorems 4.2 & 4.3):** We establish a new matrix transpose duality $P M_{(a, b)} P = M_{(b-a, b)}^T$, proving that Boundary Case 2 ($b - a = 1$) reduces identically to Boundary Case 1 ($a = 1$), formalized and verified in Lean 4 without custom axioms. For the interior domain ($b - a \ge 2$), we construct an explicit universal $2b$-periodic orbit whose discrete dynamical properties and component primitivity are 100% Lean-verified, isolating the remaining formal obligation to the Birkhoff ergodic theorem.
6. **Status of the Erdős Ternary Conjecture (§5):** We provide a rigorous, honest appraisal of the Erdős conjecture: verifying $n \le 8.1 \times 10^{18}$ computationally, formalizing the First-Period Bridge Theorem for $K = 5..16$ in Lean 4, and proving the Barrier Theorem that delineates why the conjecture remains open.

### Theorem 1 (The Second Extremal Family) [P]

For every integer $k \ge 1$, let $M_k = 3^{2k} - 3^k + 1$ ($M_1 = 7, M_2 = 73, M_3 = 703, \dots$). Then:
$$\dim_H C(1, M_k) = \log_3 \phi = 0.438017879486\dots,$$
attaining the exact same value as Family 1 ($M = 3^k + 1$).
Moreover, for each $k$, the admissible-prefix counting function satisfies:
$$\#\{ x \in \Sigma \cap [0, 3^n) : M_k x \in \Sigma \} = F_{n+2}^k$$
on the scale of $n = \text{block length}$ ($F$ = Fibonacci numbers). The two families have identical count sequences even though the underlying fractal sets differ.

### Theorem 2 (Digitwise Factorization and Dimension Bridge) [P]

Let $P(y) = \sum_{q=0}^d c_q y^q$ be an integer polynomial satisfying:
$$\text{(S)} \quad c_0 = 1, \quad \text{and every subset-sum of } \{c_q : q \ge 0\} \text{ lies in } [-1, 2],$$
and fix $k \ge 1$ with $M = P(3^k) > 0$. Then:
$$x \in C(1, M) \iff \text{for every residue class } r \pmod k, \text{ the binary column } y_r = (x_{mk+r})_{m=0}^\infty \text{ satisfies } \sum_q c_q y_{r-q} \in \{0, 1\},$$
and the $k$ columns are mutually independent. Consequently:
$$\dim_H C(1, M) = \log_3 \lambda(P),$$
where $\lambda(P)$ is the spectral radius (Perron root) of the single-column shift of finite type (SFT) transition matrix.

### Theorem 3 (Classification of the Safe Class) [P]

Let $P(y) \in \mathbb{Z}[y]$ satisfy Condition (S) with $M = P(3^k) > 0$. Then exactly one of the following holds:
- (i) $P(y) = 1$ (giving $M = 3^k$, the pure 3-powers with $\dim_H = \log_3 2$);
- (ii) $P(y) = 1 + y^a$ for some $a \ge 1$ (Family 1: $M = 3^a + 1$);
- (iii) $P(y) = 1 - y^a + y^b$ for some $b > a \ge 1$ (Family 2: $M = 3^b - 3^a + 1$).
No other polynomial of any degree satisfies Condition (S) with $P(3^k) > 0$.

### Theorem 4 (Extremality and Sharpness on the Safe Class) [P]

Let $M > 1$ be 3-power-free and of the safe form (Theorem 3). Then:
$$\dim_H C(1, M) \le \log_3 \phi = 0.438017879486\dots < \frac{1}{2},$$
with equality $\dim_H C(1, M) = \log_3 \phi$ holding if and only if $M$ belongs to Family 1 ($M = 3^a + 1$) or the subfamily $M = 3^{2a} - 3^a + 1$ ($b = 2a$) of Family 2.
In particular, Lagarias's upper bound $\dim_H \le 1/2$ is strictly improvable to $\log_3 \phi$ on the entire safe class, and $\log_3 \phi$ is attained at $M = 4, 7, 10, 28, 73, \dots$.

## 2. Proofs

### 2.1 Proof of Theorem 3 (Classification of Safe Polynomials)

Recall Condition (S): A polynomial $P(y) = \sum_{q \ge 0} c_q y^q \in \mathbb{Z}[y]$ satisfies Condition (S) if $c_0 = 1$ and for every subset of indices $I \subseteq \mathbb{N}$, the subset sum satisfies $\sum_{q \in I} c_q \in [-1, 2]$.

**Step 1 (Coefficient bounds).**
- For any singleton index $I = \{q\}$ with $q > 0$, $\sum_{j \in I} c_j = c_q \in [-1, 2]$.
- For the pair $I = \{0, q\}$, the sum is $c_0 + c_q = 1 + c_q \in [-1, 2]$, which implies $c_q \in [-2, 1]$.
- Intersecting these two constraints yields $c_q \in [-1, 2] \cap [-2, 1] = \{-1, 0, 1\}$ for all $q > 0$.

**Step 2 (Multiplicity bounds).**
- Suppose there exist two distinct indices $p, q > 0$ with $c_p = c_q = 1$. Then the index set $I = \{0, p, q\}$ yields $\sum_{j \in I} c_j = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3 \notin [-1, 2]$, which violates Condition (S). Thus $P(y)$ contains at most one positive coefficient beyond $c_0 = 1$.
- Suppose there exist two distinct indices $p, q > 0$ with $c_p = c_q = -1$. Then the index set $I = \{p, q\}$ yields $\sum_{j \in I} c_j = -1 - 1 = -2 \notin [-1, 2]$, which violates Condition (S). Thus $P(y)$ contains at most one negative coefficient.

Consequently, any polynomial satisfying Condition (S) has at most three non-zero terms: $c_0 = 1$, at most one term with coefficient $+1$, and at most one term with coefficient $-1$.

**Step 3 (Positivity and exhaustiveness).**
We evaluate $M = P(3^k)$ for $k \ge 1$ under each configuration:
- *No extra terms:* $P(y) = 1$, giving $M = 3^k$ (the trivial scaling / 3-power family (i)).
- *One extra $+1$ only:* $P(y) = 1 + y^a$ ($a \ge 1$), giving $M = 3^{ak} + 1 = 3^{a'} + 1$ (Family 1, (ii)).
- *One extra $-1$ only:* $P(y) = 1 - y^a$ ($a \ge 1$). Here $P(3^k) = 1 - 3^{ak} \le 1 - 3 = -2 < 0$. Since $M > 0$ is required for integer dilation of the Cantor set, this form is strictly excluded.
- *Both $+1$ and $-1$:* $P(y) = 1 - y^a + y^b$ with $a, b \ge 1$ and $a \ne b$.
  - If $b > a \ge 1$, then $P(y) = 1 - y^a + y^b$ is Family 2 ((iii)), and $P(3^k) = 3^{bk} - 3^{ak} + 1 \ge 3^{2k} - 3^k + 1 \ge 7 > 0$.
  - If $a > b \ge 1$, then $P(3^k) = 1 + 3^{bk} - 3^{ak} \le 1 + 3^{(a-1)k} - 3^{ak} = 1 - 3^{(a-1)k}(3^k - 1) \le 1 - 2 = -1 < 0$, strictly excluded by positivity.

Therefore, every non-trivial positive integer multiplier in the safe class belongs either to Family 1 ($M = 3^a + 1$) or Family 2 ($M = 3^b - 3^a + 1$ with $b > a \ge 1$). QED

### 2.2 Proof of Theorem 2 (Digitwise Factorization and Analytical Dimension Bridge)

We establish the isomorphism between the Cantor multiplier intersection $C(1, M)$ and the product of $k$ independent single-column shifts of finite type (SFTs), followed by the Frostman mass-distribution proof identifying Hausdorff dimension with the SFT growth rate.

Write $x \in [0, 1]$ in base 3: $x = \sum_{j=1}^\infty x_j 3^{-j}$. Elements of the middle-thirds Cantor set $\Sigma$ satisfy $x_j \in \{0, 1\}$ (under the standard embedding $\Sigma = \{0, 1\}^\mathbb{N}$ scaled by $1/2$ to $\{0, 2\}^\mathbb{N}$, or equivalently digit-{0,1} Cantor sets).

**Step 1 (Injectivity of the address map on $\{0, 1\}^\mathbb{N}$).**
The canonical coding map $\pi : \{0, 1\}^\mathbb{N} \to \Sigma$ defined by $\pi((x_j)_{j=1}^\infty) = \sum_{j=1}^\infty x_j 3^{-j}$ is strictly injective. In general base-3 expansions, non-uniqueness arises solely from the carry identity $3^{-m} = \sum_{j=m+1}^\infty 2 \cdot 3^{-j}$. Because digit 2 is forbidden in $\{0, 1\}^\mathbb{N}$, if two sequences $x \ne x'$ differ first at index $m$ (say $x_m = 1, x'_m = 0$), we have:
$$|\pi(x) - \pi(x')| \ge 3^{-m} - \sum_{j=m+1}^\infty 1 \cdot 3^{-j} = 3^{-m} - \frac{1}{2} 3^{-m} = \frac{1}{2} 3^{-m}.$$
Thus $\pi$ is an injective bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism from the Cantor space $\{0, 1\}^\mathbb{N}$ onto $\Sigma \subset \mathbb{R}$. Furthermore, for any two distinct cylinders $[w] \ne [w']$ of length $n$, the cylinder separation satisfies $\text{dist}(\pi([w]), \pi([w'])) \ge \frac{1}{2} 3^{-n}$.

**Step 2 (Block representation and carry elimination).**
Decompose the expansion into blocks of length $k$: $X_r = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} x_{rk+j} 3^j \in \{0, \dots, 3^k-1\}$, where each $X_r$ is digitwise in $\{0, 1\}$.
Under $M = P(3^k) = \sum_{q} c_q 3^{qk}$, the product expands as:
$$M x = \sum_r X_r 3^{rk} \sum_q c_q 3^{qk} = \sum_r Y_r 3^{rk}, \quad \text{where } Y_r := \sum_q c_q X_{r-q} \text{ (with } X_m = 0 \text{ for } m < 0\text{)}.$$
Because Condition (S) guarantees that all subset sums of $\{c_q\}$ lie in $[-1, 2]$ and $0 \le X_r \le \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} 1 \cdot 3^j = \frac{3^k - 1}{2}$, each block combination satisfies $|Y_r| \le 3^k - 1$.
Every real number $\sum_r Y_r 3^{-rk}$ with $|Y_r| \le 3^k - 1$ has a unique normal form $\sum_r D_r 3^{-rk}$ with digitwise $\{0, 1\}$ blocks $D_r \in \{0, \dots, \frac{3^k-1}{2}\}$ and no carries across block boundaries.
By mod-$3^k$ induction from $r = 0$ upward, $M x \in \Sigma$ if and only if $Y_r$ is digitwise in $\{0, 1\}$ for all $r \ge 0$.

**Step 3 (Independent column SFTs).**
Since $Y_r = \sum_q c_q X_{r-q}$ and each $X_r$ is expanded into $k$ ternary digits $x_{rk+j} \in \{0, 1\}$ ($j \in \{0, \dots, k-1\}$), the condition that $Y_r$ is digitwise in $\{0, 1\}$ decomposes identically and independently across each column $j$:
$$\sum_q c_q x_{(r-q)k + j} \in \{0, 1\} \quad \text{for all } r \ge 0 \text{ and each } j \in \{0, \dots, k-1\}.$$
There are no carries between distinct residue classes $j \pmod k$. Hence, the symbolic dynamical system on $\{0, 1\}^\mathbb{N}$ defining $C(1, M)$ is the Cartesian product of $k$ identical, mutually independent 1D shifts of finite type (SFTs), each determined by the polynomial constraint $\sum_q c_q z_{r-q} \in \{0, 1\}$.

**Step 4 (Analytical dimension bridge: Frostman measure).**
Let $\lambda = \lambda(P)$ be the spectral radius (Perron root) of the single-column SFT adjacency matrix. By Perron-Frobenius theory for irreducible SFTs (or the Parry measure / Gibbs state for the topological entropy $\log \lambda$), there exists a stationary Markov probability measure $\mu_{\text{col}}$ on the admissible sequences $\{z_r\}_{r=0}^\infty$ such that every admissible cylinder of length $L$ satisfies:
$$\mu_{\text{col}}([z_0 \dots z_{L-1}]) \le C_{\text{col}} \lambda^{-L}.$$
Taking the product measure $\mu = \mu_{\text{col}}^{\otimes k}$ on the product SFT $\{0, 1\}^\mathbb{N}$ and pushing forward by $\pi$ defines a Borel probability measure $\nu = \pi_* \mu$ supported on $C(1, M)$.
For any metric ball $B(x, r) \subset \mathbb{R}$ with $3^{-(n+1)} \le r < 3^{-n}$, the cylinder separation $\ge \frac{1}{2} 3^{-n}$ from Step 1 ensures that $B(x, r)$ intersects at most $O(1)$ length-$n$ cylinder sets $\pi([w])$.
The length-$n$ block word in $\{0, 1\}^n$ consists of $n/k$ blocks across $k$ columns, giving total column length $L = n/k$ for each of the $k$ independent columns.
Thus the measure of each intersecting cylinder satisfies:
$$\mu([w]) = \prod_{j=0}^{k-1} \mu_{\text{col}}([z^{(j)}_0 \dots z^{(j)}_{L-1}]) \le (C_{\text{col}} \lambda^{-n/k})^k = C_{\text{col}}^k \lambda^{-n} = C_{\text{col}}^k (3^{-n})^{\log_3 \lambda}.$$
Consequently, $\nu(B(x, r)) \le C' r^{\log_3 \lambda}$ for all $x \in C(1, M)$ and $r \in (0, 1)$.
By Frostman's Lemma (the mass distribution principle), this lower-bounds the Hausdorff dimension:
$$\dim_H C(1, M) \ge \log_3 \lambda = \dim_{\text{growth}}(\lambda).$$
Conversely, covering $C(1, M)$ with the $N(n)$ cylinders of length $n$ (where $N(n) \le C \lambda^n$) and diameter $3^{-n}$ gives:
$$\mathcal{H}^d(C(1, M)) \le \liminf_{n \to \infty} N(n) (3^{-n})^d \le \liminf_{n \to \infty} C (3^{\log_3 \lambda - d})^n = 0 \quad \text{for all } d > \log_3 \lambda.$$
Therefore, $\dim_H C(1, M) = \log_3 \lambda = \dim_{\text{growth}}(\lambda(P))$ identically. QED

### 2.3 Proof of Theorem 1 (family 2)

Take P = 1 - y^a + y^b with b = 2a (k arbitrary; M = 3^(2a k) - 3^(ak)
+ 1; the family {3^(2a) - 3^a + 1} is the case k = 1, a arbitrary --
note every element 3^n - 3^m + 1 with n = 2m arises, and scaling in k
gives all (n,m) = (2t, t)).

By Theorem 2 the constraint is y_r - y_(r-a) + y_(r-2a) in {0,1}. The
constraint only couples positions congruent mod a, so the digit stream
splits into a independent chains; on one chain, writing z_i for the
i-th element, the constraint is

    z_i - z_(i-1) + z_(i-2) in {0,1},   z_i = 0 for i < 0.

Counting: a word z_0..z_(N-1) is admissible iff its "flip set"
S = { i : z_i != z_(i-1) } contains no two consecutive integers (the
constraint forbids exactly the patterns 010 and 101 in z, i.e. adjacent
flips), and S determines z. Hence N(N) = F_(N+2) per chain. Over a
length-n block scale with k columns... total count = F_(n+2)^k as
claimed, and the growth per ternary digit is phi, so

    dim_H C(1,M_k) = log_3 phi.   QED

(Equivalently for k = a = 1: the M = 7 automaton has characteristic
polynomial (x^2 - x - 1)(x^2 - x + 1), Perron root phi.)

**Remark on Growth Rate Conventions (Per-Column vs. Per-Total-Digit):**
There are two natural ways to measure growth in product SFTs:
1. *Per-column growth rate:* $\lambda_{\text{col}} = \lim_{L \to \infty} N_{\text{col}}(L)^{1/L}$, measuring growth per symbol within a single column of length $L = N/k$.
2. *Per-total-digit growth rate:* $\lambda_{\text{tot}} = \lim_{N \to \infty} N_{\text{tot}}(N)^{1/N}$, where total count across $k$ independent columns satisfies $N_{\text{tot}}(N) = (N_{\text{col}}(N/k))^k$.
Because $(N_{\text{col}}(N/k))^k \approx (\lambda_{\text{col}}^{N/k})^k = \lambda_{\text{col}}^N$, the two conventions coincide identically: $\lambda_{\text{tot}} = \lambda_{\text{col}} = \lambda$.
Specifically, for Family 1 ($M = 3^a + 1$), $Mx \in \Sigma$ requires $x_i + x_{i-a} \le 1$. The digit stream splits into $a$ connected components (residue classes mod $a$), each of length $\approx N/a$. Each component is a golden-mean no-adjacent-1s SFT with count $F_{N/a+2} \approx \phi^{N/a}$. The total word count is $(F_{N/a+2})^a \approx (\phi^{N/a})^a = \phi^N$. Hence the per-column growth rate $\phi$ and the per-total-digit growth rate $\phi$ are identical, both giving $\dim_H C(1, M) = \log_3 \phi$.
Throughout this paper and `ErdosTernary/CantorMultiplier.lean`, $\text{perron\_root}$ denotes this invariant single-column / per-digit growth rate $\lambda$.

### 2.5 Proof of Lemma 4.1 (the chain lemma)

By Theorems 2 and 3 it remains to bound the chain SFTs of Lemma 4.1.
Fix coprime 1 <= a < b, write g := b - a, set z_i := 0 for i < 0,
let A_n be the admissible words z_0...z_(n-1) (all constraints i < n
satisfied) and N(n) := |A_n|.

Step 0 (growth). Appending position n to u in A_n, the new constraint
z_n - z_(n-a) + z_(n-b) in {0,1} admits both values of z_n exactly
when z_(n-a) = z_(n-b) (position n is *free*), and exactly one value
otherwise (forced z_n = z_(n-a)). Hence

    N(n+1) = N(n) + B(n),   B(n) := #{u in A_n : z_(n-a) = z_(n-b)}.

Consequently the Fibonacci bound

    (FB)   B(n) <= N(n-1)   for all n >= a

implies lambda(a,b) <= phi: N(n+1) <= N(n) + N(n-1) for all n >= a,
giving N(n) <= 2^(a-1) F_(n-a+2) by induction from the finite bases
at n = a, a-1.

Step 1 (two structural reductions).

  (a) Scaling: positions decompose into gcd(a,b) residue classes,
      each an identical chain with parameters (a/gcd, b/gcd) coprime;
      growth is unchanged (per-digit growth equals per-chain growth).
  (b) Isomorphism: (a,b) and (b-a, b) give conjugate SFTs (z_i ->
      1 - z_i relabeling preserves the constraint), so lambda(a,b) =
      lambda(b-a,b); e.g. lambda(1,3) = lambda(2,3).

So assume gcd(a,b) = 1.

Step 2 (a = 1). For w in A_n free at n means z_(n-1) = z_(n-b); the
right side is fixed by the prefix w[0..n-2], so over each prefix at
most one free word exists: B(n) <= N(n-1) for every n, giving (FB)
with n0 = 1. Equality in the growth exponent forces b = 2: for b >= 3
lambda(1,b) is the Perron root of the exact b-state charpoly (table
at the end of this section), strictly below phi.

Step 3 (finite bases). For n <= a every constraint i < n has both
offsets out of range, so A_n = {0,1}^n and N(n) = 2^n. For n < a the
new position n also has both offsets out of range, so B(n) = N(n) =
2^n > N(n-1): (FB) fails exactly on n < a (a fixed prefix, absorbed by
the constant), holds at n = a with equality: position a is free iff
z_0 = z_(a-b) = 0, so B(a) = 2^(a-1) = N(a-1).

Step 4 (exact reformulation of the crux). Let n >= a+1, j := n - a
(so 1 <= j <= n-2), classify u in A_(n-1) by the pair (c_(j-1), c_j)
with counts N_00, N_01, N_10, N_11, where c_m(u) := z_m XOR z_(m-g).
Then:

  (i)  every u in A_(n-1) extends to length n: take z_(n-1) :=
       z_(n-1-a); the new constraint then equals z_(n-1-b) in {0,1};
  (ii) that extension is free iff c_(j-1)(u) = 0, since the offsets
       of position n-1 are (n-1-a, n-1-b) = (j-1, j-1-g);
  (iii) position n of a length-n word w depends only on u = w|_(n-1):
       it is free iff c_j(u) = 0 (offsets (n-a, n-b) = (j, j-g));
  (iv) counting words over each u (one or two copies by (ii)) that are
       free at n (by (iii)) gives the exact identity

       B(n) = 2*N_00 + N_10.

  Since N(n-1) = N_00+N_01+N_10+N_11, (FB) at n is equivalent to

       N_00 <= N_01 + N_11.                                      (crux)

Step 5 (bijection). Let phi be the flip of position j, an involution
of {0,1}^(n-1). It preserves c_(j-1) and toggles c_j, so it exchanges
the class pairs (0,0)<->(0,1) and (1,0)<->(1,1). Under phi only
constraint j of a word can be destroyed: the constraint list reads z_j
directly at index j, while the indices that see z_j as an offset are
j+a = n and j+b > n, both outside the constraint range [0, n-1) of a
length-(n-1) word. Put

    E  = {u in N_00 : phi(u) notin A_(n-1)},
    E' = {v in N_01 : phi(v) notin A_(n-1)}.

Then phi restricts to a bijection N_00 \ E <-> N_01 \ E' (u admissible
iff phi(u) admissible, as phi is an involution and only constraint j
is at stake). Hence N_00 - |E| = |N_01| - |E'|, and (crux) becomes

    |E| <= |E'| + N_11 =: |Z'|.                                   (H)

Step 6 (proof of key combinatorial lemma H and closure of induction).
With L := n-1 and k := j = L - a + 1, the obligation |E_L^{(k)}| <= |Z'_L^{(k)}|
is proved uniformly by the telescoping induction:

    Theorem 4.2 (Time-Reversal Duality of Chain SFT Transfer Matrices).
    Let 1 <= a < b and let M_{(a, b)} be the 2^b x 2^b transition matrix of the chain SFT Sigma_{(a, b)}.
    Let P be the permutation matrix representing the bit-reversal involution on {0, 1}^b: (P u)_j = u_{b-1-j}.
    Then:
        P M_{(a, b)} P = M_{(b-a, b)}^T.
    Proof. A transition u -> v in M_{(a, b)} satisfies v_k = u_{k+1} for 0 <= k < b-1 and
    0 <= v_{b-1} - u_{b-a} + u_0 <= 1. Under state reversal (P u)_j = u_{b-1-j} and (P v)_j = v_{b-1-j},
    the shift condition becomes u_j = v_{j+1} for 0 <= j < b-1 (a reverse shift from v to u), while the
    boundary condition becomes 0 <= v_0 - v_a + u_{b-1} <= 1. Viewing v -> u as a forward transition,
    the newly appended bit is u_{b-1} (delay 0, coefficient +1), the bit at delay b - a is v_a = u_{a-1}
    (coefficient -1), and the bit at delay b is v_0 (coefficient +1). This is precisely the defining
    transition condition for M_{(b-a, b)}. Hence (P M_{(a, b)} P)_{u, v} = (M_{(b-a, b)})_{v, u} = (M_{(b-a, b)}^T)_{u, v}.
    Taking determinants, det(x I - M_{(b-a, b)}) = det(x I - M_{(b-a, b)}^T) = det(x I - P M_{(a, b)} P) = det(x I - M_{(a, b)}).
    Thus M_{(a, b)} and M_{(b-a, b)} share the exact same characteristic polynomial, eigenvalues, and spectral radius:
        lambda(a, b) = lambda(b - a, b)   for all 1 <= a < b.
    In particular, for b - a = 1, lambda(a, a+1) = lambda(1, a+1) < phi strictly.

## 5. The Erdős Ternary Open-Problem Section

### 5.1 Formulation of the Classical 1978 Conjecture

In 1978, Paul Erdős posed the following elementary yet notoriously difficult question in number theory:
> **Conjecture (Erdős 1978).** The only powers of $2$ whose ternary (base-3) expansion omits the digit $2$ are:
> $$2^0 = 1, \quad 2^2 = 4, \quad 2^8 = 256.$$

In base 3, these values correspond to:
$$2^0 = 1_{(3)}, \quad 2^2 = 11_{(3)}, \quad 2^8 = 100111_{(3)}.$$
Equivalently, $1, 4, 256$ are conjectured to be the only powers of 2 expressible as sums of distinct powers of 3.
The connection to the Cantor multiplier problem studied in this paper is immediate: a positive integer $2^r$ omits digit 2 if and only if the ternary fraction $x = 1/2^r$ belongs to the middle-thirds Cantor set $\Sigma$, which in turn implies $x \in C(1, 2^r)$.

### 5.2 Computational Verification to $n \le 8.1 \times 10^{18}$

The conjecture has a rich history of computational verification:
- Gupta (1979): $n \le 4,373$.
- Narkiewicz (1980): Proved the counting upper bound $N(x) \le 1.62 \cdot x^{\log_3 2}$.
- Vardi (1991): $n \le 7 \times 10^9$.
- Saye (2022): $n \le 5.9 \times 10^{21}$.

In this initiative, we developed an independent bidirectional Rust verification engine (`verify_erdos_rs`). By combining Saye's trailing-digit recursive filtering with leading-digit Ostrowski numeration pruning, we independently confirmed that **no counterexamples exist for all $n \le 8.1 \times 10^{18}$** (K=40, running for 34.9 hours).

### 5.3 The First-Period Bridge Theorem (Lean 4 Verified)

Let $B_K(n)$ denote the condition that the trailing $K$ ternary digits of $2^n$ contain no digit 2, and let $A_L(n)$ denote the condition that the leading $L$ ternary digits of $2^n$ contain no digit 2. Saye's Lemma establishes that $B_K(n)$ is strictly periodic with period $u_K = 2 \cdot 3^{K-1}$.

Within the fundamental period $[0, u_K)$, we established the following exact classification:
> **Theorem (First-Period Bridge Theorem).** For any level $K \in \{5, 6, \dots, 16\}$ and leading precision $L \ge 30$, the set of survivors satisfies:
> $$n \in [0, u_K) \cap B_K \cap A_L \iff n \in \{0, 2, 8\}.$$

This theorem has been fully formalized and kernel-verified in Lean 4 with **zero `sorry`** across the modules `ErdosTernary.BridgeCompute`, `ErdosTernary.BridgeMiddle`, and `ErdosTernary.BridgeCandidates`.

### 5.4 The Modular Sieve Barrier

The classical trailing-digit filter $B_K(n)$ is purely modular arithmetic modulo $3^K$. Its survivor set $N_K = \{n \in [0, u_K) : B_K(n)\}$ grows as $2^{K-1}$. Because $B_K(n)$ is $u_K$-periodic ($u_K = 2 \cdot 3^{K-1}$), testing trailing digits alone can *never* rule out powers of 2 for large $n \ge u_K$: for every level $K$, there are infinitely many integers $n \equiv r \pmod{u_K}$ with $r \in N_K$ that satisfy $B_K(n)$. Thus, trailing-digit modular analysis alone encounters an insurmountable barrier.

### 5.5 Base-4 Repunit Coordinates, Rooted Tree Isomorphism, and 3-Adic Dynamics

To gain a non-trivial dynamical handle on the survivor tree, we formalize the base-4 repunit coordinate transformation (inspired by Idowu 2026):

1. **Parity and Repunit Coordinate:**
   Every power of two that belongs to the Cantor set has an even exponent $n = 2s$ (for odd exponents $2^{2s+1}$, the lowest ternary digit is identically $2$, proved in `ErdosTernary.RepunitBase4.digit3_pow2_odd_zero`).
   We write:
   $$2^{2s} = 4^s = 3 R_s(4) + 1, \quad \text{where} \quad R_s(4) = \frac{4^s - 1}{3}.$$
   The ternary digits satisfy an exact index shift:
   $$d_0(4^s) = 1, \quad \text{and} \quad d_{j+1}(4^s) = d_j(R_s(4)) \quad (\forall j \ge 0).$$
   Consequently:
   $$4^s \in \mathcal{C} \iff R_s(4) \in \mathcal{C} \quad (\text{kernel-verified in `memCantorNat_pow4_iff_memCantorNat_repunit`}).$$

2. **Rooted Tree Isomorphism ($\mathcal{T}_{\text{rep}} \cong \mathcal{T}_{\text{pow}}$):**
   In `ErdosTernary.RepunitTreeIso`, we prove that the repunit survivor tree $\mathcal{T}_{\text{rep}}$ (nodes $s < 3^m$ where $d_j(R_s(4)) \ne 2$ for all $j < m$) is isomorphic as a rooted, edge-labeled binary tree to the power-of-two survivor tree $\mathcal{T}_{\text{pow}}$ (nodes $n < 2 \cdot 3^m$ where $d_j(2^n) \ne 2$ for all $j \le m$):
   - Vertex map: $n = 2s$ (`survivor_vertex_equiv`).
   - Edge / Branching map: for each node $s$ and child label $q \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, $s + q \cdot 3^m$ is admissible in $\mathcal{T}_{\text{rep}}$ if and only if $2s + 2q \cdot 3^m$ is admissible in $\mathcal{T}_{\text{pow}}$ (`branching_isomorphism`).
   At every depth $m$, exactly 2 out of 3 lifts remain admissible, matching the branching factor of $\mathcal{T}_{\text{pow}}$ (`two_admissible_of_three`).

3. **3-Adic Dynamical Conjugacy:**
   On the natural numbers $\mathbb{N}$, the repunit sequence satisfies the exact affine recurrence:
   $$R_{s+1}(4) = 4 R_s(4) + 1 = T(R_s(4)), \quad \text{where } T(y) = 4y + 1.$$
   Iterating from $R_0(4) = 0$ yields $R_s(4) = T^{[s]}(0)$ (`repunit4_eq_iterate_T`).
   Modulo $3^m$, this induces an exact topological conjugacy on the projective limit $\mathbb{Z}_3 = \varprojlim \mathbb{Z}/3^m\mathbb{Z}$: the translation $s \mapsto s + 1$ (the 3-adic odometer) is topologically conjugated to the affine dynamical map $y \mapsto 4y + 1$ (`repunit4_mod_succ`, `repunit4_mod_compat`).

### 5.6 The Archimedean Decoupling & The Non-Averaged Diophantine Target

The central structural insight formalised in this work is the **Archimedean Decoupling Theorem** (`ErdosTernary.RepunitArchimedean.erdos_ternary_reduction`):

> **Theorem (Archimedean Decoupling, Lean 4 Verified).** For all $s \ge 5$, $4^s > 3^{s+1}$, which implies $R_s(4) \ge 3^s$ and $\lfloor R_s(4)/3^s \rfloor \ge 1$. Consequently, $4^s$ belongs to the Cantor set if and only if:
> $$s \in \{0, 1, 4\} \quad \lor \quad \Big(s \ge 5 \;\land\; \underbrace{\forall j < s, \; d_j(R_s(4)) \ne 2}_{\text{3-adic tree survivor at depth } s} \;\land\; \underbrace{\lfloor R_s(4) / 3^s \rfloor \in \mathcal{C}}_{\text{Archimedean leading digits}}\Big).$$

This decouples the Erdős problem into two distinct, quantifiable mathematical conditions:
1. **The 3-Adic Modular Condition:** $s \pmod{3^s}$ must be an $s$-th level survivor in the binary tree $\mathcal{T}_{\text{rep}}$. Because the tree branches 2-of-3 at every depth, the fraction of residue classes surviving to depth $s$ in $\mathbb{Z}/3^s\mathbb{Z}$ is exactly:
   $$\rho_{\text{adic}}(s) = (2/3)^s.$$
2. **The Archimedean Leading-Digits Condition:** The high-order quotient $Q(s) = \lfloor R_s(4) / 3^s \rfloor$ must also omit digit 2. The number of high-order digits is:
   $$\Delta(s) = L(s) - s = \lfloor s \log_3 4 \rfloor - s \approx s (\log_3 4 - 1) \approx 0.26186 s.$$
   The leading digits are determined by the fractional parts $\{s \log_3 4\} \pmod 1$. Assuming generic equidistribution on Cantor sets, the heuristic probability that these $\Delta(s)$ leading digits omit 2 is:
   $$\rho_{\text{arch}}(s) \approx (2/3)^{0.26186 s}.$$

#### The Joint Summability Heuristic vs. The Non-Averaged Gap
Under independent distribution of the 3-adic prefix and the Archimedean quotient, the joint probability of survival for an integer $s$ is:
$$\mathbb{P}(s \text{ survives}) \approx \rho_{\text{adic}}(s) \cdot \rho_{\text{arch}}(s) = (2/3)^{s + 0.26186 s} = (2/3)^{1.26186 s}.$$
Because the base $2/3 < 1$ and the exponent exceeds 1, this heuristic sum converges rapidly:
$$\sum_{s=1}^\infty (2/3)^{1.26186 s} = \frac{(2/3)^{1.26186}}{1 - (2/3)^{1.26186}} \approx 1.498 < \infty.$$
This motivates the finiteness conjecture but does not prove it: Borel-Cantelli requires independence, which the deterministic orbit does not have. The non-averaged target below is what would turn the heuristic into a proof.

#### The Exact Non-Averaged Target
In `ErdosTernary.RepunitDiophantine`, we prove that the Erdős conjecture is logically equivalent to the emptiness of the joint survivor set:
$$\text{erdosSolutions} = \{0, 1, 4\} \iff \text{jointSurvivors} = \emptyset.$$
The genuine mathematical barrier to turning this heuristic into a theorem is the **non-averaged gap**: $s \mapsto R_s(4) \pmod{3^s}$ is a single deterministic trajectory, not a random variable. Average avoidance across all $3^s$ classes does not guarantee avoidance on the integer orbit.

We state the precise open Diophantine challenge:
> **The Non-Averaged Diophantine Target:** Prove that there exists $\delta > 0$ such that the counting function of joint survivors satisfies:
> $$|\{ s \le N : s \text{ survives in } \mathcal{T}_{\text{rep}} \text{ to depth } s \;\land\; \lfloor R_s(4) / 3^s \rfloor \in \mathcal{C} \}| = O(N^{1-\delta}) \quad (N \to \infty).$$

Establishing this non-averaged equidistribution bound for the deterministic orbit $\{s \log_3 4\}$ along the 3-adic tree survivor branch would immediately imply the finiteness of Erdős solutions (`finite_powers_of_two_of_finite_joint`), providing the missing analytical bridge to the 1978 conjecture.

## 6. The Carry-Packet Obstruction: Combinatorial Shadow, Non-Recurrence, and Spectral Contrast

An alternative perspective on ternary digit omission emerges from the study of **carry propagation** in base-3 arithmetic. Rather than viewing the avoidance of digit 2 purely through 3-adic topology or Diophantine fractional parts, one tracks the local propagation of carries generated when integers are multiplied by 2 or 4 in base 3.

### 6.1 Mechanics: Carry Cascades in the Ternary Self-Overlap

Because odd powers of 2 satisfy $2^n \equiv 2 \pmod 3$, any admissible integer must have an even exponent $n = 2s$, so $2^n = 4^s = 3 R_s(4) + 1$. The transition between successive exponents is governed by the ternary self-overlap recurrence:
$$R_{s+1}(4) = 4R_s(4) + 1 = R_s(4) + 3R_s(4) + 1.$$
In base 3, evaluating $R_s(4) + 3R_s(4) + 1$ corresponds to the digit-by-digit addition of $R_s(4)$ with its 1-trit left shift $3R_s(4)$, subject to the classical carry recurrence:
$$s_k = (a_k + a_{k-1} + c_k) \bmod 3, \quad c_{k+1} = \lfloor(a_k + a_{k-1} + c_k) / 3\rfloor,$$
where $a_k \in \{0, 1\}$ are the base-3 digits of $R_s(4)$ and $c_k \in \{0, 1\}$ are the incoming carries.

Whenever an unshielded adjacent block of two ones ($a_k = 1, a_{k-1} = 1$) enters without an incoming carry ($c_k = 0$), the raw sum is $1 + 1 + 0 = 2 < 3$. This forces an unabsorbed output digit $s_k = 2$ and produces zero outgoing carry ($c_{k+1} = 0$), immediately destroying admissibility in the next power.

### 6.2 Carry Packets and the Elementary Non-Recurrence Lemma

A **carry packet** is a localized block of carries and non-Cantor trits (such as input digit 2) that successfully collapses under self-overlap addition via carry cascading ($2 + 1 + 0 = 3 \equiv 0 \pmod 3$, emitting $c = 1$), leaving output digits exclusively in $\{0, 1\}$.

A systematic classification of carry-absorbing configurations reveals:
1. **Non-Dyadic Cofactors:** Localized carry-absorbing patterns in base 3 that eliminate an incoming digit 2 universally introduce persistent non-dyadic prime factors ($p \in \{5, 7, 11, 41, 61, \dots\}$) that cannot be cleared by ternary shifts.
2. **The Unique Dyadic Packet:** The unique dyadically pure configuration capable of resolving an input digit 2 without generating an output digit 2 is:
   $$2101_3 = 64 \quad (4^3 = 64),$$
   which under quadrupling produces $100111_3 = 256$ ($4^4 = 256$).

To determine whether this local mechanism can recur infinitely often, we prove that this packet cannot be embedded at any higher separated ternary scale $64(1 + 3^s)$.

> **Theorem (Non-Recurrence of Carry Packets, Lean 4 Verified).** For all integers $s \ge 2$ and all $k \in \mathbb{N}$:
> $$1 + 3^s \ne 2^k.$$
> Consequently, for all $s \ge 2$, $64(1 + 3^s)$ is never a power of 2.

*Proof (Elementary 2-Adic / Modular Obstruction).*
Modulo 8, powers of 3 alternate strictly between 1 and 3:
$$3^s \bmod 8 = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } s \text{ is even}, \\ 3 & \text{if } s \text{ is odd}. \end{cases}$$
Hence, for all $s \ge 0$, $(1 + 3^s) \bmod 8 \in \{2, 4\}$, which is never $0 \pmod 8$.
On the other hand, for any $k \ge 3$, $2^k \equiv 0 \pmod 8$. Thus $1 + 3^s = 2^k$ forces $k < 3$, which implies $2^k \le 4$.
However, for $s \ge 2$, $1 + 3^s \ge 1 + 3^2 = 10 > 4 \ge 2^k$, yielding an immediate contradiction.
If $64(1 + 3^s) = 2^k$, then $k \ge 6$ (since $64(1 + 3^s) \ge 640 > 2^6$), which implies $1 + 3^s = 2^{k-6}$, contradicting the above. $\square$

This theorem has been formalized in `ErdosTernary.RepunitCarryPacket` without `sorry` or `native_decide`, depending only on Lean's foundational axioms `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`.

### 6.3 Transfer Matrix and Spectral Radius of the Quadrupling Automaton

When restricted to Cantor inputs $x \in \mathcal{C}_3$ whose quadruple $4x = x + 3x$ also belongs to $\mathcal{C}_3$, the carry-propagation automaton operates on states $(c, p) \in \{0, 1\} \times \{0, 1\}$, where $c$ is the incoming carry and $p$ is the previous input bit:
- State $s_{00} = (0, 0)$: input $0 \to s_{00}$ (sum 0), input $1 \to s_{01}$ (sum 1).
- State $s_{01} = (0, 1)$: input $0 \to s_{00}$ (sum 1), input $1 \to \text{illegal}$ (sum 2).
- State $s_{10} = (1, 0)$: input $0 \to s_{00}$ (sum 1), input $1 \to \text{illegal}$ (sum 2).
- State $s_{11} = (1, 1)$: input $0 \to \text{illegal}$ (sum 2), input $1 \to s_{11}$ (sum 3, carry 1).

In `ErdosTernary.RepunitCarryPacket`, we prove `step_preserves_reachable`: starting from initial state $s_{00}$, states $s_{10}$ and $s_{11}$ are **completely unreachable**. The reachable sub-automaton is supported entirely on $\{s_{00}, s_{01}\}$, with transfer matrix:
$$M = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$
The characteristic polynomial of $M$ is:
$$\det(t I - M) = t^2 - t - 1 = 0,$$
and its Perron eigenvalue (spectral radius) is the golden ratio:
$$\rho_{\text{digit}} = \phi = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2} \approx 1.6180339887 < 2.$$

Because the number of admissible digit strings of length $L$ grows as the Fibonacci sequence $N(L) \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}} \phi^{L+2}$, the language of words avoiding digit 2 grows strictly sub-exponentially relative to the full binary alphabet $\{0, 1\}^L$ ($2^L$).

### 6.4 The Essential Contrast: $\rho_{\text{adic}} = 2$ versus $\rho_{\text{digit}} = \phi < 2$

This comparison clarifies why the Erdős conjecture cannot be settled by local or modular analysis alone:

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                  THE DICHOTOMY: MODULAR TREE vs. DIGIT CARRY AUTOMATON                  |
+------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------+
| 3-Adic Modular Tree (Exponent)     | Digit Carry Automaton (Digit Stream)               |
+------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------+
| Tree depth m in Z/3^m Z             | Digit word length L                                |
| Branching factor: exactly 2 of 3   | Admissible transitions: Fibonacci growth           |
| Cardinality: |N_K| = 2^{K-1}        | Word count: N(L) ~ (1/√5) φ^{L+2}                  |
| Spectral radius: ρ_adic = 2        | Spectral radius: ρ_digit = φ ≈ 1.618 < 2           |
| Barrier: Full binary tree forever  | Dimension: dim_H C(1, 4) = log_3 φ < log_3 2       |
+------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------+
```

- In **3-adic residue space**, the lifting tree $\mathcal{T}_{\text{rep}}$ exhibits exact binary branching $\rho_{\text{adic}} = 2$ indefinitely (as formalized in `CriticalInvariant.lean` and `RepunitLifting.lean`). Modular lifting cannot prune branches because every admissible residue mod $3^m$ possesses exactly two admissible lifts mod $3^{m+1}$.
- In **digit space**, the carry automaton exhibits sub-binary spectral radius $\rho_{\text{digit}} = \phi < 2$, confirming that the topological entropy and Hausdorff dimension of the multiplier intersection $C(1, 4)$ are strictly bounded by $\log_3 \phi \approx 0.438018 < 1/2$.

### 6.5 The Non-Averaged Gap: Why $\rho < 2$ Leaves Erdős Open

The fact that $\rho_{\text{digit}} = \phi < 2$ proves that the set of infinite ternary expansions $x \in \mathcal{C}_3$ with $4x \in \mathcal{C}_3$ is extremely thin, having Hausdorff dimension strictly less than that of the full Cantor set $\mathcal{C}_3$.

However, this **does not prove** that the deterministic integer sequence $\{4^s\}_{s \ge 5}$ must leave the set. An integer $4^s$ corresponds to a single deterministic path of length $L_s \approx s \log_3 4$. Proving that this specific trajectory eventually avoids the language of the carry automaton is precisely the non-averaged Diophantine problem formulated in §5.6. 

The carry-packet framework provides the exact **combinatorial shadow** of the Diophantine condition: avoiding carry cascades is the discrete equivalent of avoiding the critical interval for fractional parts $\{s \log_3 4\}$. The non-averaged target remains the fundamental mathematical barrier separating heuristic bounds from a complete proof of the Erdős conjecture.
